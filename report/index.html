What senior ML jobs ask for

Skills named in currently open machine learning engineer, AI engineer and research engineer postings

This report reads 2253 open, senior-eligible postings from 218 employers, fetched on 10 November 2026, and counts which of 146 skills each one names. The skills are grouped into 14 areas. Every skill listed here has review questions on the skill pages and in the review app.

Percentages are the share of postings whose description names the skill. A posting names a skill when its text matches that skill’s patterns in tools/taxonomy.py; the method and limits section says what that does and does not measure.

Summary

  • Three different jobs share these titles. Machine learning engineer postings ask for PyTorch (46%), the path to production (67%) and MLOps (44%). AI engineer postings ask for agents (75%), agent frameworks and harnesses (40%), backend services (41%) and TypeScript (24%), and only 14% name PyTorch. Research engineer postings ask for experiment rigor (60%), fast and correct research code (71%) and reinforcement learning (42%).
  • LLMs are the default subject. 68% of all postings name LLMs or foundation models and 57% name agents. Classical ML is named far less often: classical models in 12%, feature engineering in 7%, statistics in 13%.
  • Evaluation is the technical skill the three tracks share most evenly. 58% of postings name model evaluation (57%, 59% and 62% by track), and 31% name eval harnesses, judges or graders specifically.
  • Production comes before modelling. Shipping to production (61%), reliability (52%), latency and throughput (48%) and monitoring (45%) are each named more often than PyTorch (30%) or any training method (fine-tuning: 19%).
  • Senior means scope. Ownership is named in 68% of postings, cross-functional work in 66%, communication in 64%, ambiguity in 45%. Technical leadership rises from 46% of postings titled senior to 83% of those titled principal.
  • Working with AI coding agents is now a named skill: 27% of postings overall and 40% of AI engineer postings.
  • Experience asked: 72% of postings state a number of years; the median is 5, and 7 for postings titled staff.
  • What postings leave out: only 13% mention data structures and algorithms, yet nearly every loop has a coding round. Section What interviews test beyond the posting maps the rounds to the areas.

What was collected

Postings come from the public job-board feeds of Greenhouse, Ashby and Lever for a list of AI labs, AI product startups, AI infrastructure companies, consumer and marketplace technology companies, robotics and autonomy companies and trading firms, plus the career sites of large employers that run their own (tools/fetch_jobs.py, tools/fetch_jobs_bigtech.py). A posting is kept when its title is a machine learning engineer, AI engineer or research engineer role (including “software engineer, ML” and “member of technical staff” with an ML subject), is not an internship, new-graduate, management, sales or hardware role, and its description names at least three ML skills.

The feeds returned 3211 matching postings, 2617 after merging the same role posted in several locations. 202 were dropped because the description names fewer than three ML skills and 126 because the title or the experience asked is below senior, leaving 2253. 74% list a United States location. The largest employers are capped at 150 postings each so that no one company dominates.

Method and limits

Counting. Each skill has a set of text patterns (tools/taxonomy.py). Equal-opportunity, benefits and company-boilerplate lines are removed before matching. A skill counts once per posting. The patterns were checked against the phrases they match and tightened where a generic word inflated a count (for example “reliable” or “integrations”).

What the share means. It is how often employers write the skill down. It understates skills that are assumed (Python, coding ability, statistics) and overstates words that are fashionable in posting language. Treat differences of a few points as noise and read the ranking, the track differences and the large gaps.

Sample. The sample is the employers in the fetch list, not the whole market. It leans to AI labs, AI startups and robotics, and to the United States. Employers that do not publish a machine-readable board are missing unless tools/fetch_jobs_bigtech.py reaches them; the run log in data/summary.json records which were fetched. Roles titled only “software engineer” or “research scientist” are outside the three tracks and are not included.

Level. “Senior-eligible” means the title says senior, staff, lead or principal, or the title has no level and the description does not ask for two years or less.

Refreshing. python tools/fetch_jobs.py, python tools/fetch_jobs_bigtech.py, python tools/analyze_skills.py, then render the site. Postings are a snapshot; every number on this page changes when the data is refreshed.
