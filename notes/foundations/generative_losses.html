Generative Losses from First Principles

1. Why Generative Losses Need Clever Abstractions

A generative model tries to represent a distribution over high-dimensional objects:

\[ x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(x), \qquad p_\theta(x)\approx p_{\mathrm{data}}(x). \]

Directly specifying and normalizing \(p_\theta(x)\) is often intractable. Modern generative losses arise by changing the problem into something observable:

  • factor a sequence into next-token conditionals
  • lower-bound an intractable latent-variable likelihood
  • compare teacher and student distributions with a chosen KL direction
  • learn a discriminator when only samples are available
  • corrupt clean data and train a denoiser

The loss is therefore inseparable from the model architecture and sampling procedure. For classification, regression, focal, margin, and ranking losses, see Discriminative Losses from First Principles.

A generative-loss design checklist

  1. Can the model evaluate normalized likelihood exactly?
  2. If not, which latent variables, factorization, critic, or corruption process makes training observable?
  3. Does the objective cover all data modes or seek a subset of them?
  4. Is training performed on data states, model-generated states, or both?
  5. Does the tractable surrogate preserve the original statistical objective?
  6. Which weighting controls gradient across tokens, timesteps, latents, or sequences?

2. KL Divergence: Direction Defines Geometry

For distributions \(p\) and \(q\),

\[ \mathrm{KL}(p\|q) = \mathbb E_{x\sim p} \left[ \log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)} \right]. \]

KL is nonnegative and asymmetric. The first argument supplies the expectation:

Objective Strongly penalizes Typical tendency
\(\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)\) \(q\) misses regions where \(p\) has mass mass covering
\(\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)\) \(q\) puts mass where \(p\) is small mode seeking

These are tendencies, not laws. Support, parameterization, optimization, and finite-sample estimators all matter.

Maximum likelihood minimizes forward KL from data to model:

\[ \mathbb E_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[-\log p_\theta(x)] = H(p_{\mathrm{data}}) + \mathrm{KL}(p_{\mathrm{data}}\|p_\theta). \]

Because samples come from data, missing a data mode is costly. But the model is not directly penalized for assigning probability to low-density regions unless normalization moves mass away from observed data.

Important“Inverse KL” usually means reverse KL

The standard terms are forward KL and reverse KL, defined relative to a chosen reference distribution. Always write the arguments. Saying only “use KL” hides the most important design choice.

3. Next-Token Prediction: Generation as Classification

An autoregressive model uses the chain rule:

\[ p_\theta(x_{1:T}) = \prod_{t=1}^{T}p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t}). \]

Negative log-likelihood becomes

\[ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{NTP}} = -\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t}). \]

The abstraction turns one sequence into \(T\) categorical prediction targets. It provides dense supervision and exact normalized likelihood even though the space of complete sequences is enormous.

What statistical objective does NTP optimize?

Under data prefixes, NTP minimizes forward KL between the data next-token distribution and the model at every prefix. This is teacher forcing: during training, \(x_{<t}\) comes from data; during generation, prefixes increasingly come from the model. This state-distribution mismatch is exposure bias.

The reduction is part of the objective:

\[ \mathcal L = -\frac{1}{\sum_i T_i} \sum_i\sum_{t=1}^{T_i} \log p_\theta(x_{i,t}\mid x_{i,<t}) \]

weights every token equally, so long sequences contribute more total training signal. Averaging per sequence defines a different population.

NTP estimates text likelihood; it does not directly optimize factuality, semantic equivalence, pass@\(k\), or user preference. Post-training objectives exist because these sequence-level goals are not identical to token likelihood.

4. Reverse KL in On-Policy Distillation

Let \(q\) be a teacher and \(p_\theta\) a student. Standard off-policy distillation often minimizes

\[ \underbrace{\mathrm{KL}(q\|p_\theta)}_{\text{forward KL}} \]

on teacher/data prefixes. Forward KL asks the student to cover probability mass assigned by the teacher.

In on-policy distillation (OPD), the student generates prefixes \(h_t\) and the teacher evaluates what the student encounters. A common local objective is reverse KL:

\[ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{OPD}}(h_t) = \mathrm{KL} \left( p_\theta(\cdot\mid h_t) \, \middle\|\, q(\cdot\mid h_t) \right). \]

For \(a_t\sim p_\theta(\cdot\mid h_t)\),

\[ r_t = \log q(a_t\mid h_t) - \log p_\theta(a_t\mid h_t) \]

acts like a dense token-level reward. The full-vocabulary KL can be computed when teacher logits are available; sampled implementations use an RL-style policy-gradient estimator.

Two independent design choices are easy to conflate:

  1. On-policy state distribution: prefixes come from the student, reducing train–inference mismatch.
  2. Reverse-KL token geometry: student probability weights the local mismatch, discouraging student modes the teacher considers unlikely.

Reverse KL can be mode seeking, lose diversity, or become unstable when the teacher is uncertain or the student reaches prefixes far outside reliable teacher behavior. TrOPD uses trust regions and forward-KL guidance in unreliable regions. The right lesson is not that one KL direction always wins, but that state distribution, support, and divergence geometry must be designed together.

5. Latent Variables: VAE and the ELBO

A latent-variable model defines

\[ p_\theta(x) = \int p(z)p_\theta(x\mid z)\,dz. \]

The marginal likelihood and posterior \(p_\theta(z\mid x)\) are generally intractable. Introduce an encoder \(q_\phi(z\mid x)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \log p_\theta(x) &= \log \mathbb E_{z\sim q_\phi} \left[ \frac{p(z)p_\theta(x\mid z)}{q_\phi(z\mid x)} \right]\\ &\ge \mathbb E_{q_\phi} \left[ \log p_\theta(x\mid z) \right] - \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z\mid x)\|p(z)). \end{aligned} \]

The right side is the evidence lower bound:

\[ \mathrm{ELBO}(x) = \mathbb E_q\log p_\theta(x\mid z) - \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z\mid x)\|p(z)). \]

The exact identity is

\[ \log p_\theta(x) = \mathrm{ELBO}(x) + \mathrm{KL} (q_\phi(z\mid x)\|p_\theta(z\mid x)). \]

Therefore a VAE minimizes negative ELBO:

\[ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{VAE}} = \underbrace{-\mathbb E_q\log p_\theta(x\mid z)} _{\text{reconstruction NLL}} + \underbrace{\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z\mid x)\|p(z))} _{\text{rate / latent regularization}}. \]

The reconstruction term is a likelihood

It is not an arbitrary similarity metric:

  • Gaussian decoder with fixed variance implies MSE up to scale.
  • Bernoulli observations imply BCE.
  • Categorical tokens imply cross-entropy.
  • Learned variance adds a log-variance term.

A \(\beta\)-VAE multiplies the KL by \(\beta\) to change the rate–distortion tradeoff, but at \(\beta\ne1\) the objective is no longer the original ELBO.

Reparameterization and posterior collapse

For diagonal Gaussian encoder,

\[ z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x)\odot\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I), \]

which moves randomness outside the differentiable path. If a strong decoder can model \(x\) without \(z\), it may choose \(q_\phi(z\mid x)\approx p(z)\) and drive the KL toward zero: posterior collapse.

6. GANs: Learn a Discrepancy from Samples

If real density is unavailable but real samples are available, learn a critic. The original GAN game is

\[ \min_\theta\max_\psi \left[ \mathbb E_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}\log D_\psi(x) + \mathbb E_{z\sim p(z)} \log(1-D_\psi(G_\theta(z))) \right]. \]

With an optimal discriminator, this corresponds to minimizing a Jensen–Shannon divergence between data and generator distributions. The critic effectively estimates a density-ratio signal.

The saturating generator objective can have weak gradients early in training. The common non-saturating alternative is

\[ \mathcal L_G = -\mathbb E_z\log D_\psi(G_\theta(z)). \]

It has the same desired equilibrium but better gradient geometry. This illustrates a recurring distinction:

Objectives can share an optimum while producing very different learning dynamics.

GAN optimization is a game, not ordinary scalar minimization. Mode collapse, oscillation, critic overfitting, and sensitivity to relative learning rates are properties of the coupled dynamics. Hinge GANs, Wasserstein objectives, and gradient penalties alter those dynamics or the discrepancy being estimated.

7. Diffusion: Turn Density Learning into Denoising

Define a known forward corruption process. A common closed form is

\[ x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I). \]

The generative model learns reverse transitions. A variational bound on \(\log p_\theta(x_0)\) decomposes into KL terms between Gaussian reverse conditionals; these reduce to weighted denoising errors. The common simplified noise-prediction objective is

\[ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{simple}} = \mathbb E_{x_0,t,\epsilon} \left[ \|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|_2^2 \right]. \]

This resembles regression MSE, but its meaning comes from the corruption process. Solving denoising tasks across noise scales learns the score/reverse process needed to generate samples.

Where design choices enter

  • predict noise \(\epsilon\), clean data \(x_0\), or velocity \(v\)
  • sample timesteps uniformly or by importance
  • retain ELBO weights or use SNR/heuristic weights
  • fix or learn reverse variance
  • diffuse pixels or learned latent codes

Dropping theoretically derived ELBO weights can improve sample quality even though it changes the likelihood objective. The statistical derivation tells us what is being approximated; empirical optimization tells us whether that particular weighting trains well.

8. Comparing the Main Abstractions

Method Observable training signal Tractable abstraction Main mismatch
NTP exact next tokens factorized likelihood data prefixes versus model prefixes; token loss versus sequence utility
OPD teacher scores on student trajectories reverse-KL dense reward teacher reliability and mode loss on student states
VAE data and encoder-sampled latent variational likelihood bound bound looseness and latent collapse
GAN real and generated samples learned discriminator discrepancy equilibrium theory versus unstable game dynamics
Diffusion clean data and synthetic noise denoising/score estimation likelihood-derived versus perceptual weighting

No universal “generation loss” exists. Each method makes a different intractable object observable.

9. Loss Weighting Is Part of the Model

Generative objectives often have the form

\[ \mathcal L = \mathbb E_{u\sim q(u)} [w(u)\ell_\theta(u)], \]

where \(u\) might be a token position, latent sample, diffusion timestep, or trajectory state. Both sampling distribution \(q\) and weight \(w\) determine the objective.

Examples:

  • token averaging gives long documents more total weight
  • KL annealing changes the VAE rate–distortion path
  • diffusion timestep sampling changes which noise scales dominate
  • OPD rollout policy changes which prefixes receive teacher supervision
  • GAN update ratios change the game dynamics

Always log components separately. A total scalar can improve while one component collapses or dominates.

10. Numerical and Estimation Stability

  • Compute token NLL with log_softmax/cross-entropy on logits.
  • Keep KL calculations in log space when probabilities are small.
  • For sampled reverse KL, distinguish an unbiased integrand estimate from a correct gradient estimator; sampling depends on student parameters.
  • Parameterize positive variances through log variance or softplus.
  • In VAEs, monitor reconstruction NLL and KL per latent dimension.
  • In diffusion, record loss by timestep/SNR rather than only the average.
  • In GANs, monitor both players and generated diversity; discriminator accuracy alone is not progress.

11. Common Failure Modes

Failure First-principles diagnosis
exposure bias training and generation induce different prefix distributions
mode collapse objective/dynamics reward a narrow generated support
posterior collapse decoder can ignore the latent while satisfying ELBO
blurry VAE samples likelihood and latent bottleneck reward conditional averaging
unstable OPD reverse-KL estimator is applied where teacher/student support differs
poor diffusion detail parameterization or timestep weighting underserves relevant SNRs
misleading total loss reduction hides token, timestep, or component imbalance
likelihood–quality gap statistical density assigns value differently from perception/task utility

Interview Questions

  • Why does maximum likelihood minimize forward KL from data to model?
  • Explain KL direction by identifying which distribution supplies the expectation.
  • Why is NTP repeated classification and also exact sequence likelihood?
  • Separate exposure bias from KL direction in OPD.
  • Why does OPD commonly use reverse KL on student-generated prefixes?
  • Derive the ELBO and explain both VAE terms.
  • Why is VAE reconstruction loss determined by decoder likelihood?
  • How can posterior collapse be an optimum rather than an implementation bug?
  • Why does the GAN non-saturating loss improve gradients without changing the desired equilibrium?
  • How does diffusion noise-prediction MSE arise from a latent likelihood bound?
  • Why do timestep sampling and weighting define part of a diffusion objective?
